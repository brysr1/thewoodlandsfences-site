faq_title: The Woodlands fence questions
faq:
  - q: Who approves fences in The Woodlands?
    a: Each village has a Residential Design Review Committee (RDRC) of resident volunteers that meets monthly. The Development Standards Committee (DSC) adopts the standards and is the only body that can grant a variance. Applications go through the Township's online Civic Access Portal.
  - q: Do I need approval to repair my fence?
    a: A repair needs approval when more than 8 linear feet is replaced, redesigned, rebuilt, or moved. Smaller like-for-like fixes generally don't. Installing or changing pool barrier gates also needs approval.
  - q: Can I use chain link?
    a: Not as a visible fence. The standards prohibit chain link and wire fencing unless it's fully screened, along with plastic, fiberglass, and unfinished cinder block. Wood, metal, stone, and masonry are acceptable.
  - q: My lot backs to the golf course or a lake. What can I build?
    a: Solid fences aren't allowed along the rear property line of golf course or lake lots. A see-through fence such as ornamental aluminum or steel, up to 6'6", is the usual answer.
  - q: Do you work in Carlton Woods?
    a: Yes. Carlton Woods is a gated community inside the Village of Sterling Ridge, and Carlton Woods Creekside is in Creekside Park. Both follow The Woodlands standards plus their own neighborhood criteria.

      <p>The Woodlands opened in 1974 as George Mitchell's master-planned community, laid out over about 25,000 acres with an ecological plan that kept the loblolly pine forest, used natural drainage channels instead of curb-and-gutter streets, and set aside greenbelts along the creeks.     <h2>What the Residential Development Standards say about fences</h2>
    <p>These are the points that shape most fence projects here, summarized from the Township's recorded 2024 Residential Development Standards (Section 2.6 and related sections). Your neighborhood may have additional criteria, so we check your lot before we quote.</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Approval first.</b> All new fences need prior written approval from your village RDRC. Repairs need it when more than 8 linear feet is replaced, redesigned, rebuilt, or relocated.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Height limits.</b> Solid fences top out at 7'6", or 8'6" along arterial, major thoroughfare, and collector streets. See-through fences top out at 6'6". Perimeter fences must be at least 4 feet.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Finished side out.</b> The finished side faces the neighbor or street. Building it the other way, or a "good neighbor" style, needs the neighbor's written consent.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Placement.</b> No fences in the right-of-way, open space reserves, or greenbelts. Solid fences stay at least 3 feet behind the front façade, and aren't allowed along the rear line of golf course or lake lots.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Materials.</b> Wood, metal, stone, and masonry are fine. Chain link and wire (unless fully screened), plastic, fiberglass, hog-wire, and unfinished block aren't. Metal posts are limited to 2-inch galvanized posts or the posts of a wrought iron fence.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Stain.</b> Wood can weather naturally. Transparent and semi-transparent stains on the Township's list are pre-approved; paint and solid stains need approval.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Trees.</b> Fence lines go around trees. Fences can't be fastened to a tree, and trees over 6 inches in diameter can't be removed for a fence.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Pools.</b> Pools, spas, and ponds deeper than 2 feet need a code-compliant barrier and an inspection.</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p class="fx-note">Summary only. The standards are long and change over time; the current recorded version and your neighborhood criteria control.</p>
