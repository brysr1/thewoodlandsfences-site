faq_title: Kingwood fence questions
faq:
  - q: Do I need a City of Houston permit for a fence in Kingwood?
    a: Kingwood has been part of the City of Houston since 1996. Houston requires a fence permit only for fences taller than 8 feet above grade, or for any masonry or concrete fence. A typical 6- to 8-foot wood or metal fence doesn't need one.
  - q: Do I still need approval from my village?
    a: Almost always. Kingwood's villages are deed restricted, and fence changes generally go through your village's architectural review before work starts. We prepare the drawing, material, and color details for the submittal.
  - q: Is all of Kingwood in Harris County?
    a: Most of it is. A small part, including part of North Woodland Hills and Kings Manor, is in Montgomery County. We check the county and flood map for your address.
  - q: My lot backs to a greenbelt. Can I fence it?
    a: Usually, but greenbelt-facing fences are where village rules tend to be most specific about style and height. We confirm your village's requirements and set the fence on your side of the property line.

    <h2>Permits, approvals, and the river</h2>
    <div class="fx-rule-box">
      <p><strong>City of Houston permit:</strong> required only for a fence over 8 feet above grade, or any masonry or concrete fence. Processing typically takes about 10 days and carries an administrative fee.</p>
      <p><strong>Village approval:</strong> separate from the city and required under most villages' deed restrictions.</p>
      <p><strong>Floodplain:</strong> Harvey damaged about 16,000 homes in the Lake Houston area, including much of Kingwood. Since 2018, Houston has required new construction in both the 100-year and 500-year floodplains to sit 2 feet above the 500-year flood elevation. A fence isn't a building, but on these lots we choose designs and footings that hold up to standing water and let it pass.</p>
    </div>
    <p>Before any digging, we request Texas 811 utility locates.</p>
