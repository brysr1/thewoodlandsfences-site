Composite fencing, Trex being the best-known brand, looks like wood without the sanding, staining, or rot. It's a popular request. But whether you can have one depends on where you live, and in The Woodlands the answer is no.

## Why not in The Woodlands

Section 2.6 of the Township's [Residential Development Standards](https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf) lists the materials a fence may be built of: **wood, metal, stone, and masonry**. Composite isn't on that list, so we don't build composite fences anywhere inside The Woodlands.

If you live there and want low maintenance, the usual answers are:

- **Cedar on steel posts**, which keeps the wood look and avoids rotting posts at the ground. See [steel vs. wood posts](/resources/steel-vs-wood-fence-posts/).
- **Ornamental steel**, which needs very little upkeep. Metal is on the allowed list.
- **Letting cedar weather naturally.** The standards allow wood fences to weather, so staining is optional. See our [stain colors guide](/resources/woodlands-fence-stain-colors/).

Our [allowed materials guide](/resources/woodlands-allowed-fence-materials/) covers the full list.

## Where composite can work

Outside the Township, the deciding rules are your HOA's or your deed restrictions, not a single list for the whole area. That's why composite can be an option in:

- **Kingwood**, where each village's deed restrictions control. See [Kingwood](/service-areas/kingwood/).
- **Humble and Atascocita** subdivisions, depending on the HOA. See [Humble](/service-areas/humble-tx/) and [Atascocita](/service-areas/atascocita/).
- **Spring** communities, depending on the HOA. See [Spring](/service-areas/spring-tx/).
- **Porter, New Caney, and Splendora**, where newer master-planned communities have their own fence guidelines.

Before you choose composite anywhere, check your HOA's architectural guidelines, or let us check them for you.

## Composite vs. cedar

If your rules allow both, here's how they compare:

- **Upkeep:** composite needs no sanding, sealing, or staining. Rinse off dirt and pollen once or twice a year. Cedar needs cleaning and restaining to keep its color.
- **Wet, shady yards:** composite doesn't rot, which helps under heavy trees and near pools and sprinklers.
- **Color:** composite fades slightly, mostly in the first six months, then holds. Cedar grays unless it's stained.
- **Cost:** composite costs more up front. See our [pricing guide](/pricing-guide/) for current ranges.
