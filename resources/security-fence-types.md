"Security fence" covers a lot of ground, from a taller backyard fence to a fenced equipment yard with a cantilever gate. The right choice depends on three questions: what you're protecting, who you're keeping out, and what your local rules allow. Here's how the options we build compare.

## The main types

### Ornamental steel

Powder-coated steel pickets are hard to climb and hard to cut, and they look good enough for a storefront, an office, or the front of a house. Because you can see through steel, it doesn't block sight lines for cameras, security patrols, or neighbors. It's also the usual choice where design rules restrict visible chain link, including much of The Woodlands. See [ornamental steel](/materials/ornamental-steel-aluminum/).

**Best for:** storefronts, offices, homes, pool areas, and sites where appearance matters.

### Chain link with barbed wire arms

Heavy-gauge chain link on commercial framework, with tension wire, bottom rails, and three-strand barbed wire arms, is the workhorse for equipment yards, storage lots, and job sites. It's practical where looks matter less than coverage. It can be galvanized or black vinyl-coated. See [chain link](/materials/chain-link/).

**Best for:** equipment and storage yards, utility sites, and industrial perimeters outside design-controlled areas.

### Welded wire mesh

Welded wire panels give a rigid, clean-looking barrier. They suit sites that want a stronger barrier without the look of a traditional chain link yard. See [industrial fencing](/industrial-fencing/).

**Best for:** warehouses, logistics sites, and perimeters that need a cleaner look than chain link.

### Metal cages

Not every security job is a perimeter. Cages protect HVAC units, generators, backflow preventers, and inventory from theft and tampering. See [security fencing](/security-fencing/).

**Best for:** equipment that's easy to steal or damage, such as rooftop and ground-level HVAC units.

### Taller privacy fences at home

For most homes, security means privacy, a fence that's hard to see over, and gates that lock. A capped cedar privacy fence at the tallest height your rules allow does most of that. In The Woodlands, solid fences can be up to 7'6" on most lots. See our [height and placement guide](/resources/woodlands-fence-rules-height-placement/).

## Security fencing for homes

A home security fence comes down to four things:

- **Height.** Go as tall as your rules allow. In The Woodlands, that's 7'6" for a solid fence on most lots and 6'6" for a see-through one.
- **No footholds.** Keep rails, braces, and gate frames on the inside, so the outside face gives nothing to step on. Keep stored items and grills away from the fence line, too.
- **Solid or narrow-gap infill.** Board-on-board cedar leaves no gaps to see through or grab. Where you need a view, ornamental steel with closely spaced pickets is hard to climb and hard to squeeze through.
- **A lockable gate.** Fit every gate with a latch that takes a padlock, and keep it locked when no one's using it.

## Gates matter most

A security fence is only as strong as its gates. On a commercial site, every vehicle should come through a gate you control:

- **Swing gates** for standard openings.
- **Slide or cantilever gates** for wide openings and tight drives with no room to swing.
- **Operators** with keypads and remotes, plus safety sensors.

We don't install card readers or call boxes. If your site needs them, we coordinate with your access-control installer. Our [gate operators page](/fence-styles/gate-operators/) covers safety and power options.

## Local rules to check

- **The Woodlands:** commercial security fences must be as inconspicuous as possible, and black vinyl-coated chain link may be approved case by case. See our [Woodlands commercial guide](/resources/commercial-security-fencing-woodlands-tx/). Residential chain link and wire are prohibited unless fully screened inside an approved solid fence.
- **HOAs elsewhere:** under [Texas Property Code Section 202.023](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/property-code/prop-sect-202-023/), an HOA can't stop an owner from installing a perimeter fence for security, but it can regulate the fence type and keep fences out of drainage easements and sidewalks. If its covenants provide for it, it can also prohibit fencing in front of the house.
- **City of Houston** (including Kingwood): a fence permit is needed for fences over 8 feet or masonry and concrete fences.
- **Floodplains:** a mapped flood zone can require a county permit in unincorporated Harris or Montgomery County.
