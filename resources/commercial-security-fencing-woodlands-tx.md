A business in The Woodlands can't put up a fence the way a business in unincorporated Harris County can. Commercial sites answer to the Township's [Commercial Planning and Design Standards](https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/commercial-planning-and-design-standards-aug-2026-recorded.pdf), recorded in August 2026. Here are the parts that matter most when you need to secure a site.

## Every fence needs approval first

Section IV.4.6 is direct: **no wall, screen, or fence of any kind may be built unless it's specifically approved.** The Township's [commercial standards page](https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/Departments/Covenant-Administration/Commercial-Standards) says all exterior improvements must be reviewed and approved by the appropriate Plan Review Committee before construction begins. Where that committee is the Development Standards Committee, applications go through the Civic Access Portal by the deadline for the meeting where they'll be heard.

Build approval time into your schedule. A security fence planned around a lease date or an inspection needs the application in early.

## Security fences should be low-key

The standards say fences used for security must be **designed and located to be as inconspicuous as possible**. In practice, that pushes most commercial security in The Woodlands toward:

- **Ornamental steel**, which secures a perimeter and reads as a design element rather than a barrier.
- **Black vinyl-coated chain link**, which the standards say **may be approved on a case-by-case basis** when the fabric, posts, and accessories are all black vinyl-coated.
- **Landscaping and placement** that keep the fence out of the main view from the street.

Bare galvanized chain link isn't on that list. Our [security fence types guide](/resources/security-fence-types/) compares the options.

## Placement limits

- **Street right-of-way:** no fence may go in the street right-of-way.
- **Curbs:** fencing needs at least **18 inches** of setback from all curbs.
- **Street-facing screens:** screening that faces a street can't extend closer to the street than the building setback line.
- **Next to homes:** the committee may require noise-absorptive fencing on sites near residential areas.

## Screening storage, loading, and equipment

Much of the fencing on a commercial site is screening rather than security. The standards require:

- **Outdoor storage** to be kept inside a closed building, with any temporary storage or containers approved and screened as the committee requires.
- **Parking, loading, and service areas** to be screened from neighboring sites and streets, with vegetation, walls, or both.
- **Emergency generators** to be screened with materials that match or complement the building.

## Dumpster enclosures

Dumpsters and above-grade grease traps visible from a street need an approved enclosure. The standards call for:

- An enclosure that **matches or complements** the main building's style and finish.
- Walls at least **6 inches taller** than the highest point of what's being screened.
- **Metal panel gates with latches**, kept closed except when someone is using them.

## Gates and access

A security fence is only as good as its gates. On commercial sites we build swing, slide, and cantilever gates and install operators with keypads and remotes. We don't install card readers or call boxes. If your site needs them, we coordinate with your access-control installer so the gate and the system work together.
