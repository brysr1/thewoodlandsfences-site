Fences in The Woodlands take a beating from wet ground, roots, and storms, so repairs are common. Before you fix one, it helps to know whether the work needs written approval from your village's Residential Design Review Committee (RDRC). The answer is in Section 1.5 of the Township's [Residential Development Standards](https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf).

## The eight-foot rule

Approval is required when **more than eight linear feet** of any fence is or has been **replaced, redesigned, reconstructed, or relocated**.

Note the "or has been." The standards look at the fence, not at one visit. Replacing seven feet this month and seven more next month doesn't get around the rule.

A few examples:

- **A few broken pickets** replaced with matching ones: under the line.
- **One leaning post** reset with the same panels: under the line.
- **A whole section** between two posts: measure your post spacing. A section that's eight feet wide is right at the line, and two sections are over it.
- **A full side of the yard** after a storm: over the line, approval needed.

## Gates on pool barriers

If your fence is part of a pool, spa, or pond barrier, the rules are tighter. Approval is required when **any gate** in a security barrier is installed, replaced, redesigned, reconstructed, or relocated, no matter how small the job.

Section 2.13 adds a new **third-party code inspection** when more than eight linear feet of a barrier fence is replaced, or any of its gates is installed, repaired, or replaced. And while any part of the barrier is down, a **temporary barrier** has to be in place. Our [pool fence rules guide](/resources/woodlands-pool-fence-rules/) covers that process.

## Color and design changes

The standards exempt some minor repairs from approval when the original work was approved, but they say that exemption **does not include fencing, roofing, and color changes**, which need a permit at the time of repair or replacement.

In practice:

- **Matching repairs** under eight feet are the low-risk case. On a partial replacement, we work with our suppliers to try to match your existing materials.
- **Changing the design**, such as switching from side-by-side pickets to board-on-board on one run, counts as "redesigned" and needs approval.
- **Changing the color** with paint, solid stain, or a tinted sealant needs approval first. Transparent and semi-transparent stains on the Township's list are pre-approved. See our [fence stain colors guide](/resources/woodlands-fence-stain-colors/).

If you aren't sure which side of the line a repair falls on, Covenant Administration can tell you. Their number is 281-210-3800.

## Good-neighbor fences

A "good neighbor" fence alternates sections so that each side gets the finished face on some panels. Building a new one normally needs your neighbor's written consent. The standards waive that consent when you're **repairing or replacing an existing** good-neighbor fence. RDRC approval still applies if the work is over eight feet.

## After a storm

Storm damage doesn't change the rules. Rebuilding more than eight feet still needs approval, and a downed pool barrier still needs a temporary barrier right away. If a storm takes out a long run, a temporary fix that keeps the yard closed while you apply is often the right first step. We can talk through what makes sense for your lot.

If you're filing an insurance claim, our [storm damage claim guide](/resources/fence-storm-damage-insurance-claim/) covers the photos and documents to gather.

## Repair or replace?

On an older fence, the eight-foot rule sometimes tips the decision. If several sections are failing, you may need approval anyway, and replacing the whole run once can make more sense than paying for one repair after another. We'll tell you which way we'd go and why.
