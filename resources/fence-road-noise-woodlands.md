A lot of homes in The Woodlands back up to a parkway, and newer streets in Porter and New Caney sit close to the Grand Parkway, I-69, or the FM roads. If tire hum and engine brakes reach your patio, a fence for road noise is a reasonable thing to want. It can help. It just won't turn a backyard on a busy road into a quiet one, and it's better to know that before you spend the money.

Here's what a wood privacy fence actually does for traffic noise, how tall the Township lets you go, and the build details that matter most.

## What a fence can do for road noise

The Federal Highway Administration has studied this for decades. Its guidance says a barrier tall enough to break the line of sight from the road to you can lower noise by about 5 decibels, with roughly 1.5 decibels more for each additional meter of height. The best highway walls reach 5 to 10 decibels, which FHWA says can cut the loudness of traffic by as much as half.

Two other points from that guidance shape what we tell homeowners:

- **Mass matters.** FHWA says barrier material should be rigid and weigh at least 20 kilograms per square meter, about 4 pounds per square foot. A single layer of cedar pickets falls well short of that, and even board-on-board, with its overlapping layer, doesn't reach it.
- **Gaps undo it.** FHWA says openings in a barrier "destroy their effectiveness." Sound goes around ends and through cracks.

So be realistic. A tall, tight wood fence won't perform like a concrete highway wall. What it does well is block the view of traffic, soften the sharper sounds, and make a patio near the fence noticeably more comfortable. For many people that's the difference that matters.

## How tall can a fence for road noise be in The Woodlands?

Height is the biggest factor you control, and the Township's Residential Development Standards set the limits:

- **Solid fences** can be up to 7'6" on most lots.
- **Along a designated arterial, major thoroughfare, or collector street**, the portion of a solid fence that runs parallel to it can go to 8'6".
- **A cap** adds two inches of allowed height.
- **Rear lines facing a public street** must sit back 10 feet or to the platted building line, whichever is greater, and nothing goes in the right-of-way or a greenbelt reserve.

Not every busy road is a designated street, so we check yours before we design the fence. Our [height and placement guide](/resources/woodlands-fence-rules-height-placement/) covers the rest of the rules. Outside the Township, in Porter, New Caney, and Splendora, your HOA's guidelines set the height and usually cap it lower.

## Build details that make the most difference

### Pick a style with no see-through gaps

Side-by-side pickets shrink as they dry and open cracks between boards. Board-on-board overlaps each joint, so it stays closed as the wood moves. Shadowbox looks finished on both sides but leaves offset gaps that let more sound slip through. Our [board-on-board vs. shadowbox guide](/resources/board-on-board-vs-shadowbox-fence/) compares the two in detail.

### Close the bottom and the ends

A rot board along the bottom seals the gap between the pickets and the ground, and the standards allow one up to six inches. Run the fence the full length of the road side and tie it into the side fences. A gate on the road side should close tight against its stops.

### Set taller posts for wind and wet ground

A taller solid fence catches more wind, so its posts work harder. On the flatter lots around The Woodlands, Porter, New Caney, and Splendora, sandy loam sits over a clay subsoil that comes close to the surface, and in wet months water perches on it. Post holes can slough in or fill with water. We never pour concrete into a wet hole. We get the water out, add a gravel base, and then set the post, deeper for a taller fence. Galvanized steel posts inside a solid wood fence are allowed in The Woodlands and hold up better in wet ground.

### Keep the trees

Trees over six inches in diameter can't be removed to make room for a fence in The Woodlands, and you wouldn't want them gone anyway. They don't stop much sound, but they screen the view and make the fence look like it belongs.

## When a fence isn't the answer

A fence helps the yard more than the house. FHWA notes barriers do little for rooms that rise above them, so a second-floor bedroom facing the road won't hear much change. For that, windows and insulation are the better fix. If the road sits well above your lot, a fence also has a hard time breaking the line of sight.
