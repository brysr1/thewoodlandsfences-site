Walk any village in The Woodlands and you'll see why the Township is protective of its trees. Most lots back to pines and hardwoods, and plenty of fence lines run right through the middle of them. The Residential Development Standards have specific rules for this, and a fence planned around them will last longer anyway.

## What the standards require

Section 2.6 of the Township's Residential Development Standards sets three rules for fences near trees:

- **Work around the trees.** "Every effort must be made to preserve existing trees" by adjusting the fence line to weave among, jog around, or abut trees and stands of trees.
- **Don't attach to trees.** No part of a fence may be fastened to a tree.
- **Don't remove larger trees for a fence.** No tree with a diameter greater than six inches, measured two feet above natural grade, may be removed to install fencing.

Tree removal falls under its own section of the standards and needs approval. When you apply for a fence, flag any tree you're asking to remove so Township staff can see it on their site visit. For projects that bring tractors or other machinery onto the lot, the standards also require construction fencing around trees in the work area and along the access route.

## How we lay out a fence line around trees

Every lot is different, but these are the habits we follow on wooded Woodlands lots:

- **Walk the line first.** Before we draw anything, we mark where trunks, large surface roots, and low limbs sit along the proposed fence line.
- **Jog the fence, don't cut the tree.** Where a trunk sits on or near the line, we route the fence around it with a short jog or an angled section, leaving room for the trunk to keep growing.
- **Move posts, not roots.** Post spacing can flex a little. When an auger hits a major root, we shift the post or dig by hand rather than cut through it.
- **Keep equipment off the root zone.** Most of a tree's roots are shallow and spread well past the trunk. We keep heavy equipment and spoil piles out of that area as much as the lot allows.
- **Leave room for the trunk.** A fence built tight against a young tree will be pushed out of line within a few years. We leave a gap and plan for growth.

## Why it matters for the fence, too

Trees and fences fight over the same ground. A post set into a root zone can be lifted as roots grow, a panel fastened near a trunk gets shoved out of line, and branches over the fence drop limbs in storms. Routing the fence around the trees from the start avoids most of these problems.

If your fence line runs through heavy tree cover, a see-through fence, such as ornamental aluminum or a split rail, can be easier to fit between trunks than a solid privacy fence. See our [Woodlands fence rules guide](/resources/woodlands-fence-rules-height-placement/) for what's allowed where.
