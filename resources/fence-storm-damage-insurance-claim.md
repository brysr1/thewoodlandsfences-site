Fence damage is a common sight after a big storm. Whether insurance helps depends on your policy, but the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) publishes plain-language guidance that answers most of the first questions. This article sums it up. It isn't legal or insurance advice, and your policy is what counts.

## Does homeowners insurance cover fence damage?

Usually, if the damage was sudden, like wind or a fallen tree, and only after your deductible. Rot, termites and normal wear usually aren't covered. Check your own policy, because coverage varies.

## Is fence damage covered?

According to TDI's [guide to covered structures](https://tdi.texas.gov/tips/home-insurance-structures-covered.html):

- **Wind:** if a fence was damaged by wind and you have wind coverage, your policy will probably pay for the damage.
- **Ice and snow:** most policies don't pay for fence damage caused by ice or snow unless the house collapses on the fence.
- **Other structures limit:** most home policies cover other structures, such as fences, at **10% of your house's insured value**. With $300,000 of coverage on the house, that's $30,000 for other structures.

## Why the check may be smaller than you expect

Two things reduce what a fence claim pays:

- **Depreciation.** TDI says policies usually pay **actual cash value** for fences, which subtracts depreciation based on age and condition. An older fence is worth less on paper than a new one costs to build.
- **Your deductible.** You still pay it. On a fence-only claim, the deductible can take a big share of the payment, so compare the two before you file.

## Fallen trees

TDI's [storm FAQ](https://tdi.texas.gov/Consumer/storms/home-damaged-faq.html) says many policies provide some coverage to remove trees or limbs that fell in a storm if they damage the house or block the driveway, but **trees and limbs falling in your yard usually aren't covered**. If a neighbor's tree falls, the neighbor generally isn't responsible for an act of nature unless they were at fault. Ask your insurer how your policy handles a tree on a fence.

## What to do first

TDI's [tips after hail or windstorms](https://tdi.texas.gov/tips/after-hail-or-windstorms.html) include:

- **File as soon as you can.**
- **Take pictures and video** of the damage before anything is moved.
- **Prevent more damage.** Your policy may pay for temporary repairs. For a fence, that might mean closing a gap so pets stay in, or putting up a temporary pool barrier.
- **Save all receipts.**
- **Don't throw damaged items away** until your insurer says you can.
- **Keep a list** of everyone you talk to at the insurance company.
- **Be there** when the adjuster comes, so they see all the damage.

If you took "before" photos ahead of storm season, include them. Our [hurricane prep checklist](/faq/hurricane-season-fence-prep/) covers that.

## Choosing who rebuilds it

TDI's contractor tips are worth following after any storm:

- **Get more than one estimate.**
- **Don't pay in full up front.**
- **Avoid anyone who offers to waive your deductible.** TDI says waiving a homeowner's deductible is illegal under state law, and your insurer may ask for proof you paid it.
- **Never sign a contract with blank spaces.**

TDI also cautions that some companies treat a claim as final and settled once you sign the check, so talk to your insurer before you sign.
