If you're replacing a fence on a shared line, the board-on-board vs. shadowbox fence question comes up fast. Both are upgrades over a plain side-by-side cedar fence, and both show up all over The Woodlands, Porter, New Caney, and Splendora. They look alike from a distance, but they're built differently, and that changes how private the yard feels and who gets the good side.

Here's how the two compare, one factor at a time.

## How each one is built

**Board-on-board** puts every picket on the same side of the rails. The first layer goes up with gaps, and a second layer covers those gaps, overlapping the boards on each side. The finished face is a solid wall of cedar, and the posts and rails show on the other side.

**Shadowbox** splits the pickets between both sides of the rails, alternating one in front and one behind. Each board covers the gap on the opposite side, so straight on, it reads as solid. Both faces look the same, and the rails sit in the middle.

## Board-on-board vs. shadowbox fence, compared

### Privacy

Board-on-board wins. Because the overlap is on one plane, there's no line of sight through the fence from any angle, and it stays that way as the cedar dries and shrinks. A shadowbox blocks most of the view straight on, but from an angle you can see slivers of the yard behind it. For a pool, a hot tub, or a patio that faces a neighbor's second-story windows, board-on-board is the safer pick. Our [privacy fence page](/fence-styles/privacy/) covers the build details.

### Looks from both sides

Shadowbox wins here. Your neighbor sees the same finished fence you do, which makes it an easy conversation on a shared side or back line. With board-on-board, one side gets the rails. In The Woodlands, the finished side has to face the street or neighboring property, and putting the unfinished side toward a neighbor needs that neighbor's written consent. So on a board-on-board fence, plan on the rails facing your own yard.

### Airflow and storms

A shadowbox lets air move between the boards. That cuts some of the wind load on a tall fence when a strong front or a tropical storm comes through, and it helps the fence and the ground along it dry out after rain. On shaded lots under the pines, where a fence can stay damp for days, that's a real advantage. Board-on-board is a closed wall, so it catches more wind, which puts more strain on the posts at the ground line.

### Wet ground and posts

The posts don't care which style hangs on them, but the ground does. On the flatter lots around The Woodlands, Porter, New Caney, and Splendora, the clay subsoil sits close to the surface, and in wet months water perches on top of it. Post holes slough in or fill with water. We never pour concrete into a wet hole; we get the water out, add a gravel base, and then set the post. For a solid board-on-board fence that catches more wind, galvanized steel posts are worth pricing. Our [steel vs. wood posts guide](/resources/steel-vs-wood-fence-posts/) walks through that choice.

### Cost

Both take more pickets and labor per foot than side-by-side. Between the two, the difference is usually small and depends on height, how much overlap is specified, and whether there's a cap. We price your fence the way your review committee or HOA will approve it.

### Staining and upkeep

A shadowbox has two finished faces and more exposed board edges, so staining both sides is more work. A board-on-board fence has one show face and a rail side, and the tight overlap can hold moisture and pine needles between boards if it never gets cleaned. Either way, the Township pre-approves the transparent and semi-transparent stains on its list.

## What the Township allows

Both styles fit the standards. A shadowbox is less than 75 percent see-through, so it counts as a solid fence, same as board-on-board. That means the solid fence limits apply to both: 7'6" on most lots, 8'6" along designated major streets, plus two inches for a cap, and no solid fence along the rear line of a golf course or lake lot. Every new fence still needs RDRC approval, and your Neighborhood Criteria may already name a style. Our [semi-privacy page](/fence-styles/semi-privacy/) has more on shadowbox designs.

## The practical takeaway

- **Choose board-on-board** if full privacy matters most, the fence screens a pool or patio, or the line faces a street where only one side is seen.
- **Choose shadowbox** if the fence sits on a shared line, the lot is shaded and slow to dry, or you'd like a little breeze to come through.
- **Either way,** ask about post type, post depth, and what happens when a hole fills with water.
