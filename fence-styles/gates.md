faq:
  - q: "Why does my new gate stick?"
    a: "As new posts settle, a gate can drift and stop latching cleanly. That's normal for a new fence, and we'll adjust it free."
  - q: "Do I need anything special for a driveway gate in The Woodlands?"
    a: "If a gate restricts vehicle access to your lot, the Township requires a Knox box or another approved way for emergency vehicles to get in, and you have to keep it working. Contact the fire department to set it up. The gate needs RDRC approval like the fence."
  - q: "Can you fix a sagging gate instead of replacing it?"
    a: "Often, yes. We re-hang it, brace it, and fit new hinges or a latch. If the gate post is rotting at the ground, the post needs replacing too. See [repair](/maintenance-repair/)."

    <h2>Solar or wired openers</h2>
    <p>An automatic opener runs on one of two power setups. We look at the drive, the sun, and the nearest power before recommending one.</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Solar.</b> A panel charges a battery at the gate, so there's no power line to trench down a long drive. It's the common pick for acreage entrances. The panel needs several hours of direct sun, so under pines it may go on a separate pole where it clears the tree line.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Wired.</b> Runs on household power. It suits heavy gates, gates that open many times a day, and entrances close to the house. The line-voltage feed is generally work for a licensed electrician, and we coordinate with yours.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Battery backup.</b> Solar keeps running on its battery during an outage. A wired opener needs a backup battery to do the same.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Manual release.</b> Every opener has one, so the gate can be opened by hand. We show you how it works at the walkthrough.</span></li>
    </ul>

</section>

    {% include eyebrow.html text="Local rules" %}
    <h2>Gate rules in The Woodlands and nearby</h2>
    <p>Section 2.6 of The Woodlands Township's <a href="https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf" rel="noopener">Residential Development Standards</a> covers gates along with fences:</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Emergency access.</b> If a gate restricts vehicle access to a lot, a Knox box or other approved emergency access has to be installed and kept working.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Height.</b> Driveway gates may be taller than the fence. Along the front of the lot, decorative gates and columns are limited to 12 inches above the fence on lots under 85 feet wide, or 8 feet on wider lots.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Side and rear yards.</b> Decorative gates and columns can go over 6 feet if they fit the house, with the top of the gate no more than 2 feet above the fence.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Pool gates.</b> Installing or changing a gate on a pool barrier needs approval and a new inspection.</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p>Outside The Woodlands, your HOA usually sets gate style and color, and acreage gates in unincorporated county usually don't need a county permit. See <a href="{{ '/service-areas/the-woodlands/' | relative_url }}">fencing in The Woodlands</a>.</p>
    <p class="fx-note">Summary only. The recorded standards and your neighborhood's criteria control, and they can change.</p>
