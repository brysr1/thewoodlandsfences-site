faq:
  - q: "Do you install card readers or call boxes?"
    a: "No. We install gate operators with keypads, remotes, and safety sensors. For card readers, call boxes, or a full access-control system, we coordinate with your access-control installer."
  - q: "Solar or wired?"
    a: "Solar avoids trenching a power line down a long drive, so it's the common pick for acreage entrances. Wired suits heavy gates, gates that open many times a day, and entrances close to the house. We look at the gate, the sun, and the nearest power before recommending one."
  - q: "What happens to an automatic gate in a power outage?"
    a: "Solar openers keep running on their battery. A wired opener needs a backup battery to do the same. Every opener has a manual release so the gate can be opened by hand."

    {% include eyebrow.html text="Safety" %}
    <h2>Safety comes with the install</h2>
    <p>An automatic gate is heavy, moving equipment. The door and gate industry association DASMA's <a href="https://www.dasma.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/GateSystemsSafetyBrochure.pdf" rel="noopener">gate safety guidance</a> references the UL 325 and ASTM F2200 standards. A few points from it shape every install:</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Monitored sensors.</b> External entrapment protection, such as photo eyes and sensing edges, must be monitored for presence and correct operation.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>A separate walk gate.</b> Automated gates are for vehicles. People need a separate walk-through entrance.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Guarded slide gates.</b> Exposed rollers and pinch points need covers, and gaps between vertical bars should be under 2¼ inches.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Controls out of reach.</b> Keypads and other access devices go at least 6 feet from any moving part of the gate.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Warning signs</b> on both sides of the gate.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Manual release.</b> Know how to cut power and open the gate by hand. We show you at the walkthrough.</span></li>
    </ul>

    {% include eyebrow.html text="Power" %}
    <h2>Solar or wired</h2>
    <p>The right power source depends on the gate's weight, how often it opens, and how far it sits from the house.</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Solar</b> stores power in a battery at the gate, which avoids trenching a line down a long drive. The panel has to see the sun for several hours a day, which under tall pines can mean mounting it on its own pole.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Wired</b> operators run on house power and handle heavier gates and frequent use. A licensed electrician generally runs the line-voltage feed, and we work around their schedule.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Backup power.</b> Solar runs on its battery in an outage. Add a backup battery to a wired operator if the gate has to keep working when the power is out.</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p>For more on gate styles and materials, see <a href="{{ '/fence-styles/gates/' | relative_url }}">gates</a>.</p>

Driveway gates may be taller than the fence, and the gate needs RDRC approval. See <a href="{{ '/service-areas/the-woodlands/' | relative_url }}">The Woodlands</a>.</p>

Your HOA usually sets gate style and color too.</p>
