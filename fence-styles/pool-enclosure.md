faq_title: Pool fence questions
faq:
  - q: "Does a pool fence need approval in The Woodlands?"
    a: "Yes. Pool barrier fences and gates need approval and a code inspection."
  - q: "Can my existing backyard fence be the pool barrier?"
    a: "Often, if it meets code. In The Woodlands, using a shared property-line fence as the barrier can mean extra inspections, and if your neighbor later replaces more than 8 feet of it, it has to be inspected again. Many owners build a separate barrier closer to the pool for that reason."
  - q: "Which way should a pool gate swing?"
    a: "Away from the pool. Walk gates should be self-closing and self-latching, and a latch lower than 54 inches goes on the pool side, near the top of the gate, where a small child can't reach it."
  - q: "What happens to the barrier while my fence is being replaced?"
    a: "A temporary barrier has to be up any time part of the permanent one is down."

    {% include eyebrow.html text="Local rules" %}
    <h2>Pool barrier rules in The Woodlands and nearby</h2>
    <p>The Woodlands requires pool barriers to meet the International Residential Code and Sections 2.6 and 2.13 of the Township's <a href="https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf" rel="noopener">Residential Development Standards</a>. In practice:</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Approval and inspection.</b> Pool barrier fences and gates need RDRC approval and a code inspection.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Changes trigger a new inspection.</b> Replacing, redesigning, rebuilding, or moving more than 8 linear feet of a barrier fence, or adding a gate, means a new inspection by a qualified third-party inspector. That's true even if your neighbor made the change.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Temporary barriers.</b> When any part of the barrier is down, a temporary one has to go up.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>During construction.</b> A continuous barricade at least 4 feet tall has to surround the pool site while it's being dug and built.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Above-ground pools.</b> These must be screened from view by a 6-foot solid fence or wall.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Height limits still apply.</b> See-through fences top out at 6'6"; solid fences at 7'6".</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p>The barrier itself follows the same basic measurements in most codes: at least 48 inches tall, no opening a 4-inch ball can pass through, and walk gates that open away from the pool and close and latch on their own. The <a href="https://cpsc.gov/s3fs-public/362%20Safety%20Barrier%20Guidelines%20for%20Pools.pdf" rel="noopener">Consumer Product Safety Commission's barrier guidelines</a> explain the details in plain language.</p>
    <p>Outside The Woodlands, your city or county code and your HOA decide what applies. We check before we quote. See <a href="{{ '/service-areas/the-woodlands/' | relative_url }}">fencing in The Woodlands</a> and <a href="{{ '/resources/woodlands-fence-approval-rdrc/' | relative_url }}">how to get a fence approved</a>.</p>
    <p class="fx-note">Summary only. The adopted code, the recorded standards, and your neighborhood's criteria control, and they can change. Your inspector has the final word on compliance.</p>
