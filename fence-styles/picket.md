faq:
  - q: "Can I put a picket fence in my front yard in The Woodlands?"
    a: "Usually not a wood one, since most wood pickets count as a solid fence there. A steel picket fence can go in the front yard if it's set back at least 5 feet from the front property line with plants in front of it. Your RDRC makes the final call."
  - q: "How tall is a picket fence?"
    a: "Most are 3 to 4 feet. In The Woodlands, a perimeter fence has to be at least 4 feet tall. A garden fence of 4 feet or less inside the yard is pre-approved if it can't be seen from the street or a neighbor's lot."
  - q: "Can a picket fence keep my dog in?"
    a: "It depends on the dog. A low picket fence works for many small dogs. For a jumper or a digger, we'll suggest a taller fence or steel with puppy pickets. See [pet fences](/fence-styles/pet/)."

    <h2>Picket fence rules in The Woodlands and nearby</h2>
    <p>In The Woodlands, Section 2.6 of the Township's <a href="https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf" rel="noopener">Residential Development Standards</a> sets the rules. The parts that matter most for a picket fence:</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Approval first.</b> Every new fence needs written approval from your village RDRC before it goes in. We prepare the drawing and material details for you.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Solid or see-through.</b> A fence that is less than 75 percent transparent counts as solid. Most wood picket fences fall in that group, so they have to sit at least 3 feet behind the front of the house or garage.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Front-yard fences.</b> See-through fencing in a front yard must be set back at least 5 feet from the front property line, with plants between the fence and the street. It can be up to 6'6" tall.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Height.</b> A perimeter fence must be at least 4 feet tall.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Color.</b> White fences are considered only where they fit the house. Paint and solid stain need approval; listed clear and semi-transparent stains don't.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Materials.</b> Wood and metal are fine. Plastic, which includes vinyl, isn't allowed.</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p>Outside The Woodlands, your HOA's deed restrictions usually decide whether a front-yard fence is allowed and what it can look like. In Kingwood, the City of Houston requires a permit only for fences over 8 feet or for masonry. In unincorporated Harris County, a residential fence outside the flood hazard area doesn't need a county permit. We check your address before we quote. See <a href="{{ '/service-areas/the-woodlands/' | relative_url }}">fencing in The Woodlands</a> and our <a href="{{ '/resources/woodlands-fence-rules-height-placement/' | relative_url }}">guide to Woodlands fence rules</a>.</p>
    <p class="fx-note">Summary only. The recorded standards and your neighborhood's criteria control, and they can change.</p>

    <p>Picket fences are short, but cost still depends on:</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Material.</b> Cedar, steel, and vinyl are priced differently per foot.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Picket style.</b> Dog-ear is the simplest. French Gothic and scalloped tops take more cutting and fitting.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Length and height.</b> More fence means more posts, rails, and pickets.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Gates.</b> Each walk gate adds a frame, hinges, and a latch.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Ground and trees.</b> Slopes, roots, and fence lines that bend around trunks add labor.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Finish.</b> Stain or approved paint adds a step after the build.</span></li>
