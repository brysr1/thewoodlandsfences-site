The Atlantic hurricane season runs from **June 1 to November 30**, according to [NOAA](https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/noaa-predicts-below-normal-2026-atlantic-hurricane-season). A fence can't be made storm-proof, but a little prep before the season makes it more likely to stand, and makes it easier to deal with if it doesn't.

## Before the season

- **Push on the posts.** Walk the fence and push near the top of each post. A post that rocks at the ground is the one most likely to go in high wind. Fix it now, before a storm finds it.
- **Fix gates and latches.** A gate that doesn't latch can swing and beat itself, and its posts, apart in the wind.
- **Look up.** Dead limbs over the fence line can come down on it in a storm. In The Woodlands, check the Township's rules before removing trees; trees larger than six inches in diameter can't be removed to make room for a fence.
- **Clear the bottom.** Soil, mulch, or debris against the bottom boards holds water and slows drainage after heavy rain.
- **Take photos.** Photograph each side of the fence while it's in good shape. If you ever file an insurance claim, "before" photos help.

## When a storm is coming

- **Latch or tie open gates** so they can't swing freely.
- **Bring in loose items** stored against the fence.
- **Pool owners:** a fence that serves as a pool barrier still has to protect the pool after the storm. Plan for a temporary barrier if part of it comes down. See our [pool fence rules guide](/resources/woodlands-pool-fence-rules/).

## After the storm

- **Stay safe first.** Watch for downed power lines before going near a damaged fence.
- **Photograph the damage** before anything is moved or cleaned up.
- **Close the gap** if pets, kids, or a pool are involved, even with a temporary fix.
- **Check the rules before rebuilding.** In The Woodlands, replacing more than eight linear feet needs approval. See our [repair approval guide](/resources/woodlands-fence-repair-approval/).
