Two things on a plat or flood map can change a fence project: a **flood zone** and an **easement**. Neither one automatically rules out a fence, but both add steps.

## Flood zones

Start with FEMA's [Flood Map Service Center](https://msc.fema.gov/portal/home). Enter your address and see whether any part of the lot is in a mapped special flood hazard area.

What that means depends on the county:

- **Unincorporated Harris County:** the county's [permit FAQ](https://oce.harriscountytx.gov/Services/Permits/FAQ) says no permit is required for a residential fence outside the special flood hazard area, and it counts fences inside the floodplain as development, which needs a permit.
- **Unincorporated Montgomery County:** work in a mapped floodplain needs a floodplain development permit. Three-strand barbed wire fences are exempt. See the county's [development regulations](https://cms1files.revize.com/montgomerycountytx/Development%20Regulations%20-%20Adopted%203-4-2025.pdf).
- **Cities:** Houston, Humble, and Splendora have their own rules. See our [permit FAQ](/faq/fence-permits-woodlands-kingwood/).

On a mapped lot, the design matters as much as the permit. An open fence lets water and debris pass. A solid fence across a low spot can act like a dam.

## Easements

An easement gives someone else, usually a utility or drainage district, the right to use part of your lot. Your survey or plat shows where they are.

- **The Woodlands:** the [Residential Development Standards](https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf) say fencing in a drainage, sanitary sewer, water line, or pipeline easement must be approved by the easement owner as well as the review committee.
- **HOAs elsewhere:** under [Texas Property Code Section 202.023](https://codes.findlaw.com/tx/property-code/prop-sect-202-023/), an HOA can prohibit fencing that obstructs a drainage easement or drainage area.
- **Utility easements:** the easement owner may need access to its lines. Ask before you build across one.

Our [property survey guide](/faq/property-survey/) covers how to find easements on your lot.
