faq:
  - q: Do I need a permit to fence my acreage?
    a: It depends on the county and the flood map. In unincorporated Montgomery County, a typical fence doesn't need a county building permit, but work in a mapped floodplain needs a floodplain development permit, and three-strand barbed wire fences are exempt. In unincorporated Harris County, a residential fence needs a permit only inside the special flood hazard area. Inside a city such as Splendora, check city rules.
  - q: Should I get a survey before fencing acreage?
    a: On a long perimeter, yes. Old fences on rural land don't always follow the true property line. A survey with the corners marked lets us build on your side of the line with confidence.
  - q: Do I have to fence my neighbor's cattle out?
    a: In much of Texas, yes. Texas is open range by default, so livestock owners generally don't have to fence animals in, and a neighbor who wants them kept out builds the fence. Counties can change that by local election, and land along U.S. and state highways is treated as closed range. Texas A&M's Five Strands handbook suggests checking local status with the sheriff, county attorney, or Extension agent.
  - q: Will a fence stop feral hogs?
    a: A well-built one helps. Texas A&M AgriLife recommends a strong 6-foot net-wire perimeter fence with the bottom touching the ground everywhere and gates closed at night, or an electric fence with at least two strands, about 8 and 18 inches off the ground.

    <h2>Who regulates a rural fence</h2>
    <div class="fx-rule-box">
      <p><strong>Unincorporated Montgomery County</strong> (most of Porter, New Caney, and the land around Splendora): a typical fence doesn't need a county building permit.
