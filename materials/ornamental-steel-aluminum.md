faq_title: Ornamental steel questions
faq:
  - q: "Aluminum or steel?"
    a: "Both look like black wrought iron from the street. Steel is heavier and stiffer, so it's the stronger choice for drive gates, street frontage, and security. Aluminum can't rust, so it's a good fit near pools and water. Many yards use both. See [aluminum fences](/materials/aluminum/)."
  - q: "Is ornamental fencing allowed on my golf course lot?"
    a: "Usually it's the required choice. We confirm your HOA or Township rules for your lot."
  - q: "Will a steel fence rust?"
    a: "Not where the powder coat is intact. Rust starts where a trimmer, mower, or falling limb chips through to bare metal. Touch up chips with paint made for powder-coated metal as soon as you see them."
  - q: "Can a steel fence be a pool barrier?"
    a: "Yes. Steel pool styles have tight picket spacing and self-closing, self-latching gates. In The Woodlands, pool barrier fences need approval and an inspection. See [pool enclosures](/fence-styles/pool-enclosure/)."

      <p>Ornamental metal is the answer wherever a solid fence isn't allowed or isn't wanted. In The Woodlands, solid fences aren't permitted along the rear line of golf course and lake lots, and see-through fences can go to 6'6".     <h2>When steel is the right metal</h2>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Drive gates.</b> A wide gate hangs a lot of weight from one post. With an automatic opener, the frame has to stay square through thousands of cycles, and steel holds its shape.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Street frontage and parking areas.</b> Where a car or mower could bump the fence, steel bends less.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Security.</b> Steel is hard to climb and hard to cut, which is why we use it for storefronts and offices too. See <a href="{{ '/security-fencing/' | relative_url }}">security fencing</a>.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Wooded lots.</b> A heavy pine limb that would bend a lighter panel is more likely to leave steel standing.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Big dogs.</b> A dog that hits the fence at a run won't flex a steel panel.</span></li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Steel fence rules in The Woodlands and nearby</h2>
    <p>Section 2.6 of The Woodlands Township's <a href="https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf" rel="noopener">Residential Development Standards</a> allows metal fences, and uses the term "wrought iron" for ornamental metal. The points that matter most:</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Height.</b> See-through fences top out at 6'6".</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Golf course and lake lots.</b> Solid fences aren't allowed along the rear line, so ornamental metal is the usual choice.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Front yards.</b> A see-through fence in a front yard has to sit at least 5 feet back from the front property line, with plants between it and the street.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Gates and columns.</b> Drive gates may be taller than the fence. Along the front of the lot, decorative columns and gates are limited to 12 inches above the fence on lots under 85 feet wide, or 8 feet on wider lots.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Posts.</b> Metal posts are fine on wrought iron fences. Stamped metal posts aren't.</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p>Outside The Woodlands, your HOA's deed restrictions usually set the style and color. In Kingwood, the City of Houston requires a permit only for fences over 8 feet. See <a href="{{ '/service-areas/the-woodlands/' | relative_url }}">fencing in The Woodlands</a> and our <a href="{{ '/resources/woodlands-fence-rules-height-placement/' | relative_url }}">guide to Woodlands fence rules</a>.</p>
    <p class="fx-note">Summary only. The recorded standards and your neighborhood's criteria control, and they can change.</p>
