faq:
  - q: "Is composite worth the price?"
    a: "If you plan to stay and hate staining, often yes. Over 10-plus years the lack of maintenance closes much of the gap with cedar."
  - q: "Can I put a composite fence up in The Woodlands?"
    a: "Maybe not. The Township's standards list wood, metal, stone, and masonry as approved materials, and composite isn't on that list. Your village RDRC makes the call, so we check before you choose it. Cedar is the usual choice there."
  - q: "Does composite need any maintenance?"
    a: "Very little. Rinse off dirt and pollen once or twice a year. In shady, damp spots, wash off any mildew before it builds up. There's no sanding, sealing, or staining."
  - q: "Will a composite fence fade?"
    a: "Yes, slightly. Most of the change happens in the first 6 months. After that, the color holds."

    <h2>Composite fence rules in The Woodlands and nearby</h2>
    <p>Section 2.6 of The Woodlands Township's <a href="https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf" rel="noopener">Residential Development Standards</a> sets the materials list:</p>

      <li><span><b>Approval first.</b> Every new fence needs written approval from your village RDRC, and the committee decides whether a product fits the rules.</span></li>

    <h2>Composite or cedar?</h2>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Up front.</b> Cedar costs much less to install.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Upkeep.</b> Cedar needs stain every few years to keep its color. Composite doesn't.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Look.</b> Cedar has real grain that changes with age. Composite keeps one even color.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Rules.</b> Cedar is allowed almost everywhere. Composite may not be, including in The Woodlands.</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p>If you can't decide, ask us to price both on the same layout.</p>
