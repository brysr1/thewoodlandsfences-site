faq:
  - q: "Will vinyl warp in Texas heat?"
    a: "Quality vinyl is formulated for UV and heat. Lower-grade product and dark colors are more prone to issues, so we use reputable product and set posts properly."
  - q: "Can I use vinyl in The Woodlands?"
    a: "No. The Township's standards prohibit plastic and fiberglass fences."
  - q: "How do I clean a vinyl fence?"
    a: "A garden hose handles pollen and dust. For mildew in shady spots, use a soft brush and mild soap, then rinse. Skip harsh scrubbers that can dull the finish."
  - q: "Is vinyl cheaper than wood?"
    a: "Not usually. Vinyl privacy fences cost more up front than wood, and even vinyl rail can run close to cedar. You save over time by skipping stain."
  - q: "Can a damaged vinyl panel be replaced?"
    a: "Usually, yes. Panels and rails slide into the posts, so a broken section can often be swapped without rebuilding the run. Matching an older color can be harder, so it helps to keep a spare."

    <h2>Vinyl fence rules in The Woodlands and nearby</h2>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>The Woodlands.</b> Section 2.6 of the Township's <a href="https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf" rel="noopener">Residential Development Standards</a> lists plastic and fiberglass among prohibited fence materials, so vinyl isn't approved.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>HOAs elsewhere.</b> Many allow vinyl, often with limits on color and where it can face the street. We check your deed restrictions before we quote.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>City of Houston.</b> A fence over 8 feet needs a permit.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Unincorporated county.</b> In unincorporated Montgomery and Harris County, a typical residential fence doesn't need a county building permit, but a mapped floodplain can require a floodplain permit.</span></li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Vinyl, cedar, or composite?</h2>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Cedar</b> costs less than vinyl or composite for a privacy fence and has real wood grain, but it needs stain every few years.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Vinyl</b> skips the stain and comes in bright, even colors. It looks clean rather than natural.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Composite</b> has the closest look to wood with almost no upkeep, and costs the most.</span></li>
    </ul>

    <h2>What affects the price</h2>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Style.</b> On our <a href="{{ '/pricing-guide/' | relative_url }}">pricing guide</a>, vinyl typically runs $25&ndash;$70 per linear foot installed. Three-rail starts at about $25&ndash;$30, and privacy at about $50.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Quality.</b> Vinyl comes in many quality levels. In Houston heat and sun, a thicker, higher-quality product costs more but adds much-needed resistance to sagging and cracking.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Height.</b> Taller panels and posts cost more.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Gates.</b> Steel-reinforced vinyl gates are priced on their own.</span></li>
