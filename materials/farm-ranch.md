faq:
  - q: "Do I need a permit for ranch fencing?"
    a: "In unincorporated Montgomery County, a three-strand barbed wire fence is exempt from floodplain development permits. Other fences in a mapped floodplain may need one. We check your property."
  - q: "What fence is best for horses?"
    a: "No-climb wire or board fence. Horses can get a hoof caught in wide mesh and can be cut by barbed wire, so we don't recommend barbed wire for horse pastures."
  - q: "Why are corners braced?"
    a: "A long run of tight wire pulls hard on the end and corner posts. An H-brace keeps those posts from leaning, so the wire stays tight for years."
  - q: "Can you clear the fence line?"
    a: "We walk the line with you first. Brush and small trees along the line add labor and are part of your quote. We'll tell you up front if the line needs heavier clearing."

    <h2>Ranch fence rules in our area</h2>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Unincorporated county.</b> In unincorporated Montgomery and Harris County, a typical fence doesn't need a county building permit.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Floodplain.</b> If the line crosses a mapped floodplain, a county floodplain permit may apply. In unincorporated Montgomery County, a three-strand barbed wire fence is exempt. We check the <a href="https://msc.fema.gov/portal/home" rel="noopener">FEMA flood map</a> for your address.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Cities.</b> Splendora is an incorporated city, so we confirm its requirements for addresses inside city limits.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>The Woodlands.</b> Wire and wire mesh fences aren't allowed unless fully screened, so ranch-style wire fencing doesn't fit there.</span></li>
    </ul>
