faq:
  - q: "How long does a cedar fence last here?"
    a: "With good posts and periodic staining, a cedar fence commonly lasts well over a decade in our climate. Posts and how they were set usually decide the lifespan."
  - q: "Cedar or pine?"
    a: "Cedar resists rot and movement better and looks better stained. Pine is cheaper. Many fences use cedar pickets with treated pine framing."
  - q: "When can I stain a new fence?"
    a: "New wood needs time to dry before it takes stain well. We'll tell you when your fence is ready. After that, many fences here benefit from a fresh coat every few years, depending on sun, shade, and sprinklers."
  - q: "What stain colors are allowed in The Woodlands?"
    a: "Wood may weather naturally, and the transparent and semi-transparent stains on the Township's list are pre-approved. Paint and solid-color stains need approval first. See [staining](/fence-staining-power-washing/)."

    {% include eyebrow.html text="Three woods" %}
    <h2>Pine, Japanese cedar, or western red cedar?</h2>
    <p>We build with all three. Each makes a good fence. The difference is price, and how the wood holds up over the years.</p>
    <div class="fx-cards">
      <div class="fx-card"><h3>Pressure-treated pine</h3><p>The lowest-cost wood fence. The pine is treated with a preservative to resist rot and insects, and it's strong, which is why we also use it for posts and rails. It starts out pale tan or slightly green and fades to gray. Treated pine moves more than cedar as it dries, so expect some shrinking and twisting in the first year. Let it dry before you stain it.</p></div>
      <div class="fx-card"><h3>Japanese cedar (sugi)</h3><p>It has a warm reddish-brown color and takes stain well. It costs not much more than pine, which is a big reason it's so popular for backyard privacy fences. It's rated moderately durable against rot, and it moves a little more than western red cedar as it dries, so a few boards may cup over time. A three-rail build and regular stain keep it looking good.</p></div>
      <div class="fx-card"><h3>Western red cedar</h3><p>The premium choice. It's rated durable to very durable against rot, and it's one of the most stable softwoods, so boards stay flatter and straighter. The color is a rich red-brown with darker streaks. It's a real step up in price, and worth it for a fence you want to look its best for years.</p></div>
    </div>
    <ul class="fx-checklist" style="margin-top:24px">
      <li><span><b>Pick pine</b> to get the most fence for the money.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Pick Japanese cedar</b> for the best value on a backyard privacy fence.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Pick western red cedar</b> for a premium look, more natural rot resistance, and fewer cupped boards.</span></li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</section>

    {% include eyebrow.html text="Local rules" %}
    <h2>Wood fence rules in The Woodlands and nearby</h2>
    <p>Section 2.6 of The Woodlands Township's <a href="https://www.thewoodlandstownship-tx.gov/files/sharedassets/main/v/2/covenant-administration/documents/2024-residential-standards-recorded-12-10-2024.pdf" rel="noopener">Residential Development Standards</a> lists wood as an acceptable fence material:</p>
    <ul class="fx-checklist">
      <li><span><b>Approval first.</b> Every new fence needs written approval from your village RDRC.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Height.</b> Solid fences top out at 7'6", or 8'6" along designated major streets, plus 2 inches for a cap.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Steel posts.</b> 2-inch galvanized steel posts are allowed on the inside of a solid fence, no taller than the fence.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Finished side out.</b> The finished side faces the street or any tract of land.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Stain.</b> Transparent and semi-transparent stains on the Township's list are pre-approved.</span></li>
    </ul>
    <p>Outside The Woodlands, your HOA's deed restrictions usually set height, style, and stain color. See <a href="{{ '/service-areas/the-woodlands/' | relative_url }}">fencing in The Woodlands</a>.</p>
    <p class="fx-note">Summary only. The recorded standards and your neighborhood's criteria control, and they can change.</p>

      <li><span><b>Posts.</b> Steel posts cost more than wood posts.</span></li>
      <li><span><b>Stain.</b> A required stain color adds a finishing step.</span></li>
